CHAPTER I  
THE PROBLEM AND ITS SCOPE
Introduction
Agriculture is the backbone of the Philippine economy, particularly in rural municipalities such as Kinoguitan in the province of Misamis Oriental. The Local Government Unit (LGU) of Kinoguitan, through its Office of the Municipal Agriculturalist (OMA), is mandated to oversee, coordinate, and promote agricultural development within the municipality. This responsibility covers crop production, livestock and poultry raising, municipal fisheries and aquaculture, agricultural producer profiles, land utilization, and other agricultural data that are critical for evidence-based planning and the effective delivery of government services to farmers, livestock and poultry raisers, fisherfolk, and aquaculture operators.
At present, the current practice of the OMA of Kinoguitan remains largely paper-based and manually driven. Agricultural data such as producer registrations, land use and crop records, livestock and poultry inventories, fisherfolk records, fish catch, and aquaculture production records are maintained through physical forms, logbooks, and separate files. This traditional approach introduces four significant and interconnected operational problems. First, the complete absence of a centralized digital data banking system means that agricultural information exists in fragmented records, making it highly vulnerable to data loss, damage, and deterioration over time. Second, the manual preparation of reports for submission to municipal and provincial offices is time-consuming and inaccurate, as staff must physically compile information from scattered sector records. This leads to delayed processing of results and error-prone submissions.
Moreover, the third problem is connected with the difficulty in retrieving and managing crop, livestock and poultry, fisheries, and aquaculture records, which hampers the ability of the office to respond quickly to data requests and monitor agricultural performance across barangays. Lastly, the absence of data validation and consistency-checking mechanisms is the fourth key problem. It allows errors and duplicate entries to accumulate in the records, further undermining the integrity and reliability of the agricultural data maintained by the office. A crop-only information structure is also insufficient for representing animal inventories, fisherfolk activities, fish catch, and managed aquaculture cycles required under the expanded operational scope of the OMA.
With the rapid advancement of information and communication technologies, government agencies at all levels are increasingly adopting digital systems to streamline their operations. The use of information systems in the public sector has been demonstrated to significantly improve data accuracy, accessibility, and organizational efficiency. Studies on local government unit-level digital transformation in the Philippines have found that the implementation of digital data management systems in municipal agriculture offices substantially reduces data inconsistencies and improves the completeness of agricultural records submitted to higher government agencies (Dela Cruz et al., 2021). Furthermore, Wolfert et al. (2023) emphasized that the shift from paper-based to digital agricultural data management has become essential for modern agricultural governance, as data banking provides the foundation for informed policy decisions at the local government level.
For the LGU of Kinoguitan, the development of AGRODATA: An Agricultural Data Management System in Agricultural Offices presents a timely and practical solution to address the four longstanding challenges of manual data management identified in this study. This study aims to design and develop a web-based agricultural data management system for the Office of the Municipal Agriculturalist of LGU Kinoguitan, Misamis Oriental, with integrated coverage of crops, livestock and poultry, municipal fisheries, and aquaculture.
      The proposed system will serve as a centralized digital platform for managing agricultural data handled by the OMA through four integrated functionalities: a unified agricultural producer and production-record system that supports farmer profiling, farm and crop yield monitoring, livestock and poultry inventory and production records, fisherfolk registration and fish catch encoding, and aquaculture site and culture-cycle monitoring; data validation and consistency-checking mechanisms that detect errors, prevent duplicate entries, enforce valid quantities and dates, and ensure the reliability of sector records; an automated report generation module that enables the Municipal Agriculturalist to produce accurate and timely reports, including the Quarterly Crop Production Report, Seasonal Farm Inventory Report, Annual Municipal Agriculture Summary, Livestock and Poultry Inventory Report, Municipal Fisheries Catch Report, and Farmer Registry, through authenticated server-side functions that query and compile data directly from the database; and an analytics and decision support module that provides descriptive statistical summaries, historical recorded trends, and interactive visual dashboards for crop, livestock and poultry, and fisheries data to support evidence-based agricultural planning and policy formulation. The final fields and formats of reports intended for external submission remain subject to validation by the OMA and the receiving offices.


Framework
      The conceptual framework of this study follows the Input-Process-Output (IPO) model, where inputs include manually managed agricultural data such as agricultural producer profiles, land use and crop records, livestock and poultry records, fisherfolk and fish catch records, aquaculture sites and culture cycles, user credentials, reporting parameters, and backup files. The process involves AGRODATA's core functions, including centralized cross-sector data storage, validated record management, automated report generation through server-side functions, descriptive data analytics and decision support, role-based access, audit logging, and backup and restoration. The outputs are accurate digital crop, livestock and poultry, fisheries, and aquaculture records; searchable producer histories; automated sector and consolidated reports; and data-driven analytical insights including statistical summaries, visual dashboards, and historical recorded trends.

Statement of the Problem
      This study aims to develop a digital agricultural data management system for the Office of the Municipal Agriculturalist of LGU Kinoguitan, Misamis Oriental to improve cross-sector data management, reporting, analytics, and decision-making processes.
Specifically, this study seeks to answer the following questions: 
1. How can a unified agricultural producer and production-record system be developed in terms of:
1.1 agricultural producer profiling,
1.2 farm, plot, planting, and crop harvest monitoring,
1.3 livestock and poultry inventory and production monitoring,
1.4 fisherfolk registration and municipal fish catch monitoring, and
1.5 aquaculture site and culture-cycle monitoring?
2. How can the system implement data validation and consistency-checking mechanisms in terms of:
2.1 error detection in crop, livestock and poultry, fisheries, and aquaculture data entries,
2.2 prevention of duplicate producer and production records, and
2.3 ensuring valid dates, non-negative quantities, and reliable relationships among sector records?
3. How can the system facilitate the Municipal Agriculturalist in generating reports for: 
3.1 crop production and seasonal farm inventory,
3.2 livestock and poultry inventory,
3.3 municipal fisheries catch,
3.4 farmer registry and annual municipal agriculture summaries, and
3.5 municipal and provincial use based on stakeholder-approved reporting requirements?
4. How can the system provide descriptive data analytics and decision support through:
4.1 statistical summaries generated through database computations,
4.2 interactive dashboards and graphical visualization, and
4.3 historical recorded trends for crop, livestock and poultry, and fisheries data?

Scope and Delimitations of the Study
      This study focuses on the design, development, and evaluation of AGRODATA, a web-based agricultural data management system for the Office of the Municipal Agriculturalist (OMA) of LGU Kinoguitan, Misamis Oriental. The system aims to address issues such as the absence of a centralized digital database, inefficient manual report preparation, difficulties in retrieving agricultural records, inconsistencies in data management, and the lack of data-driven analytics for agricultural planning across crop, livestock and poultry, fisheries, and aquaculture activities.
      AGRODATA includes modules for a unified agricultural producer registry, agricultural land and farm-plot records, crop catalog and planting and harvest monitoring, livestock species and periodic inventory and production records, fisherfolk profiles and municipal fish catch records, and aquaculture sites and stocking-to-harvest cycles. The existing Farmers table serves as the shared producer record so that one person may be linked to crop, livestock and poultry, fisheries, or aquaculture activities without creating separate identities. The system also features automated report generation through Supabase Edge Functions for the Quarterly Crop Production Report, Seasonal Farm Inventory Report, Annual Municipal Agriculture Summary, Farmer Registry, Livestock and Poultry Inventory Report, and Municipal Fisheries Catch Report. These reports are compiled from validated database records; any format intended for official external submission remains subject to confirmation by the OMA and the receiving office. Data validation mechanisms detect missing or invalid entries, enforce non-negative quantities, prevent inconsistent harvest dates, and preserve relationships among producers and sector records. Furthermore, the analytics module provides descriptive SQL-based statistical summaries, dashboards, barangay and species comparisons, and historical recorded trends for crops, livestock and poultry, and fisheries.
      The system is intended solely for authorized OMA personnel, including the Municipal Agriculturalist and office staff. Farmers, livestock and poultry raisers, fisherfolk, and aquaculture operators will not directly access or operate the system but may benefit indirectly through improved agricultural data management and service delivery. The study is limited to a web-based platform and does not include a mobile application, online public access, or automatic integration with regional and national agricultural information systems.
      The study is limited to administrative record management and descriptive statistical analysis based on encoded agricultural data. It does not perform predictive analytics, machine learning, or production forecasting. It also excludes veterinary diagnosis and treatment, laboratory and biosecurity case management, fisheries licensing and enforcement, vessel tracking, individual-animal traceability, commercial accounting, and market-price management.


Significance of the Study
The development of the AGRODATA for LGU Kinoguitan is expected to provide significant benefits to the following stakeholders:
Municipal Agriculturalist and OMA Staff. The system directly resolves the identified problems of the office. The Municipal Agriculturalist and OMA staff will benefit from a centralized digital platform that eliminates fragmented paper-based record keeping, enables fast retrieval of crop, livestock and poultry, fisherfolk, fish catch, and aquaculture records, and automates sector and consolidated report generation through server-side functions. The analytics and decision support features will provide SQL-computed descriptive summaries, interactive dashboards, and historical recorded trends, enabling the Municipal Agriculturalist to make informed decisions regarding agricultural programs, resource allocation, and policy recommendations.
      Agricultural Producers. Crop farmers, livestock and poultry raisers, fisherfolk, and aquaculture operators in the municipality will benefit indirectly from the system, which is operated by authorized OMA personnel. By improving the organization and accuracy of cross-sector agricultural data, the system supports better program targeting and more efficient delivery of government services and interventions. Unified producer records also reduce repeated encoding when a person participates in more than one agricultural sector.
      Local Government Unit of Kinoguitan. The LGU will benefit from improved data management capabilities that support evidence-based policy formulation and agricultural planning. Timely crop, livestock and poultry, fisheries, and aquaculture information will provide municipal officials with a broader description of local food-production activities and support strategic planning and resource allocation decisions.
      Provincial Agriculture Office and Partner Agencies. The Provincial Agriculture Office and relevant partner agencies may receive more accurate, consistent, and timely agricultural summaries from the municipality. Report fields, classifications, schedules, and formats intended for external submission shall be validated with the concerned office before the system claims formal compliance.
      Future Researchers. This study will serve as a valuable reference for future researchers and capstone proponents developing information systems for government agencies, particularly those addressing integrated crop, livestock and poultry, fisheries, and aquaculture data banking, records management, automated reporting, and descriptive analytics for decision support.

Output of the Study
The study will develop a fully functional, web-based AGRODATA for the Office of the Municipal Agriculturalist of LGU Kinoguitan, Misamis Oriental. The system will replace fragmented manual processes with a centralized and secure PostgreSQL database that stores unified agricultural producer profiles; farm, plot, planting, and harvest records; livestock species, inventories, and production records; fisherfolk and fish catch records; and aquaculture sites and culture cycles. It will incorporate an automated report generation module implemented through authenticated Supabase Edge Functions that query, aggregate, and format production records into the Quarterly Crop Production Report, Seasonal Farm Inventory Report, Annual Municipal Agriculture Summary, Farmer Registry, Livestock and Poultry Inventory Report, and Municipal Fisheries Catch Report. Final external reporting formats and signatories will remain subject to stakeholder validation. Search, filtering, pagination, and validated data-entry forms will enable efficient retrieval and maintenance of records across all covered sectors.
      Furthermore, the system will feature an agricultural analytics dashboard that transforms recorded agricultural data into descriptive statistical summaries through automated SQL-based computations. The analytics module executes aggregation queries against the PostgreSQL database to compute totals, averages, counts, and distributions across barangays, crops, livestock and poultry species, fisheries subsectors, and reporting periods. The resulting values are stored in crop, livestock, and fisheries statistical-summary tables and displayed through interactive charts. The dashboard presents crop area and yield indicators, livestock and poultry inventory by species, municipal fish catch by subsector, aquaculture harvest totals, barangay-level comparisons, and historical recorded trends to support evidence-based agricultural planning and decision-making.

Definition of Terms
The following terms are defined as used in this study:
Agricultural Data Banking. The systematic digital collection, storage, and organization of all agricultural data managed by the OMA, replacing paper-based record keeping with a centralized database system.
Agricultural Analytics. The process of summarizing recorded agricultural data using descriptive statistics such as totals, averages, counts, and percentages, computed through SQL-based aggregation queries executed against the database, and rendered as graphical visualizations through the system's charting library to support agricultural planning. 
Agricultural Produce. Refers to the crops and agricultural products harvested within the municipality, including recorded information on planting activities, harvests, yield data, and other crop-related agricultural records managed and analyzed through the AGRODATA system.
Agricultural Producer. A person recorded by the OMA who participates in one or more agricultural activities, including crop farming, livestock or poultry raising, municipal fishing, or aquaculture. The implemented Farmers record serves as the unified producer profile for these activities.
Livestock and Poultry Inventory. A dated record of the number of livestock or poultry maintained by a producer, organized by animal species, barangay, and reporting period, together with births, deaths, dispositions, and applicable production quantities.
Fisherfolk. An agricultural producer engaged in municipal capture fishing whose activity profile may include barangay, level of involvement, vessel type, and gear type.
Fisheries. The sector covered by AGRODATA that includes marine municipal fishing, inland municipal fishing, and managed aquaculture production records within the approved scope of the OMA.
Fish Catch Record. A dated record of a fisherfolk's catch that identifies the fisheries subsector, species name, quantity, unit, and relevant remarks.
Aquaculture. The managed culture of aquatic organisms in a pond, cage, tank, pen, or other approved production site.
Aquaculture Cycle. A stocking-to-harvest record for an aquatic species at an aquaculture site, including stocking date and quantity, harvest date and quantity when available, unit, and cycle status.
Automated Report Generation. The system functionality that compiles and formats agricultural data through authenticated server-side database query functions. The implemented reports include the Quarterly Crop Production Report, Seasonal Farm Inventory Report, Annual Municipal Agriculture Summary, Farmer Registry, Livestock and Poultry Inventory Report, and Municipal Fisheries Catch Report. Formats intended for external submission remain subject to stakeholder validation.
Dashboard. A visual display interface that presents key performance indicators, descriptive statistical summaries, and graphical representations of crop, livestock and poultry, fisheries, and aquaculture data, enabling quick comprehension of recorded information.
Decision Support System. A component of the information system that provides SQL-computed descriptive statistical summaries, interactive dashboards, historical recorded trend visualizations, and sector reports that assist the Municipal Agriculturalist in evidence-based decision-making.
Farmer Profile or Unified Agricultural Producer Profile. A comprehensive identity and registration record for a person engaged in one or more covered agricultural sectors. It links the producer to applicable farm and crop, livestock and poultry, fisherfolk and fish catch, and aquaculture records without creating separate identities.
Information System. A set of interconnected components that collect, process, store, and communicate data and information in support of decision-making and organizational management.
      Agricultural Data Management System in Agricultural Offices (AGRODATA). The web-based information system developed in this study for the Office of the Municipal Agriculturalist of LGU Kinoguitan for centralizing and managing all agricultural data, including monitoring, reporting, analytics, and decision support.
Local Government Unit (LGU). Refers to the Municipal Government of Kinoguitan, Misamis Oriental, which is responsible for local governance and delivery of basic services within its jurisdiction.
Municipal Agriculturalist. The government official designated by the LGU who is responsible for planning, implementing, and coordinating agricultural programs and services within the municipality.
Office of the Municipal Agriculturalist (OMA). The office within the LGU is tasked with agricultural development, extension services, and management of agricultural data and programs.
Provincial Agriculture Office Reports. Agricultural reports prepared for municipal or provincial use based on validated database records. Their fields, schedules, classifications, signatories, and layouts must be confirmed with the OMA and the receiving office before formal compliance is claimed.
      Statistical Summary. A compilation of computed values including totals, averages, distributions, and other descriptive statistics derived from agricultural data to provide quantitative insights.
      Trend Analysis. The examination of historical data patterns over time, performed by querying time-series agricultural production records in the database and rendering the resulting data as line or bar charts, to identify directions, patterns, and changes in agricultural production metrics.
 Web-Based Application. A software application accessed through a web browser over a computer network such as the Internet or a local area network (LAN).

CHAPTER II  
REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES
This chapter presents a review of related literature and studies that provide theoretical and empirical support for the development of the AGRODATA: An agricultural data management system in agricultural offices for the Office of the Municipal Agriculturalist of LGU Kinoguitan, Misamis Oriental. 


Agricultural Data Banking Systems
The concept of agricultural data banking refers to the centralized digital collection, storage, and management of all agricultural records within a government office or agency. Wolfert et al. (2023) emphasized that the shift from paper-based to digital agricultural data management systems has become essential for modern agricultural governance, noting that data banking provides the foundation for precision agriculture and informed policy decisions at the local government level. Their review demonstrated that local agricultural offices that adopted digital data banking systems experienced measurable improvements in data accuracy, completeness, and accessibility compared to those still relying on paper records.
In the Philippine context, the Provincial Agriculture has been actively promoting the digitization of agricultural records through various national programs. The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA, 2023) has repeatedly emphasized that incomplete and inconsistent local agricultural data poses a significant challenge to national food security planning. Studies on LGU-level digital transformation in the Philippines have found that the implementation of digital data banking systems in municipal agriculture offices significantly reduced data inconsistencies and improved the completeness of agricultural records submitted to higher government agencies (Dela Cruz et al., 2021).
Batoon et al. (2023) developed a Farm Data Management System for the municipal agriculture office in San Ildefonso, which addressed the same core problem of the absence of a digital data banking system. Their study found that the system enabled the agriculture office to maintain a comprehensive and current database of registered farmers, landholdings, and crop production activities, and significantly reduced the time required for data management tasks. These findings directly support the first objective of the present study, which aims to develop a centralized digital data banking system for the OMA of LGU Kinoguitan.


Automated Report Generation in Government Agricultural Offices
The preparation of reports for submission to higher government agencies such as the Provincial Agriculture is a critical function of municipal agriculture offices. Kumar et al.  (2023) conducted a study on a web-based agricultural information system for a district agriculture office in India and found that automated report generation reduced the time required to produce official agricultural produced reports by seventy percent compared to the manual process. Their study also found that automated reports were significantly more accurate than manually prepared ones, as they drew directly from the system database rather than relying on manual compilation from scattered paper records.
Gonzales et al. (2022) developed a web-based agricultural produced monitoring system for a provincial agriculture office in Mindanao, Philippines, and specifically highlighted the importance of automated report generation features in reducing the administrative burden on agricultural officers. Their study found that the system's report generation module enabled the office to produce consolidated production reports in a fraction of the time previously required, and that the accuracy and consistency of the reports improved substantially. These findings are directly relevant to the second objective of the present study, which aims to design and implement an automated report generation module for the OMA of LGU Kinoguitan.
Radhakrishnan (2025) further noted that one of the most impactful benefits of e-government information systems in agricultural offices is the elimination of the bottleneck caused by manual report preparation, which often results in delayed submissions to oversight agencies. The automation of routine reporting tasks frees agricultural officers to focus on more substantive activities such as field extension work and farmer support programs.


Agricultural Produce Records Management
Effective management of agricultural produce records is fundamental to the operations of any municipal agriculture office. Basir et al. (2024) emphasize that on-farm recordkeeping is essential in agricultural digitalization, as it provides critical data for economic analysis and informed decision-making. Their study highlights the transition from traditional paper-based recordkeeping to digital systems that integrate various tools such as farm management software, web applications, and mobile applications. However, they also note that these systems vary widely in data recording methods, which can lead to challenges in achieving complete, accurate, and interoperable data. This underscores the importance of implementing efficient and well-structured digital recordkeeping systems that enhance data quality, accessibility, and interoperability in agricultural management.
Moreover, Zhang et al. (2020) explored cloud-based agricultural data management systems in China and found that centralized produced records management enabled provincial agriculture departments to improve coordination and generate comprehensive agricultural statistics. Their study highlighted that without a structured digital records management system, agricultural data becomes fragmented and inconsistent over time, making it increasingly difficult to retrieve accurate historical produce data for planning purposes. These findings support the third objective of the present study, which aims to implement a produce records management module with advanced search and retrieval capabilities.
In the local context, Dela Cruz et al. (2021) developed an agricultural management information system for a municipality in Nueva Ecija, Philippines, and specifically found that the system's records management module resolved the problem of delayed and inconsistent data retrieval that had previously characterized the office's manual system. Their evaluation confirmed that OMA staff were able to locate specific farmer and produce records significantly faster using the digital system compared to the manual filing system.


Agricultural Analytics and Decision Support Systems
The integration of data analytics and decision support capabilities in agricultural information systems represents a significant advancement in modern agricultural governance. Htun et al. (2022) emphasized the importance of developing visual-assisted decision support systems in agriculture, noting that dashboards and graphical representations of agricultural data enable officials to quickly comprehend complex information and make informed decisions. Their study found that visual analytics significantly improved the ability of agricultural officers to identify trends, anomalies, and patterns in production data that would otherwise remain hidden in tabular reports.
Kumar et al. (2023) highlighted that information and communication technologies in agriculture have evolved beyond simple data storage to encompass analytical capabilities that transform raw data into actionable insights. Their comprehensive review demonstrated that agricultural information systems with integrated analytics features, including statistical summaries and trend analysis, substantially improved the quality of agricultural planning at the local government level. These systems enabled agricultural officers to move from reactive to proactive management approaches.
Garcia Juan (2026) conducted a socio-technical systems analysis of agricultural enterprises and found that digital systems with analytical capabilities are essential for supporting evidence-based decision-making in agricultural management. The study emphasized that statistical analysis and trend visualization help agricultural managers identify production patterns, optimize resource allocation, and anticipate potential challenges before they escalate.
Furthermore, Davis and Granić (2024), in their comprehensive review of technology acceptance models, noted that systems offering tangible benefits such as data-driven insights and analytical dashboards demonstrate higher user adoption rates compared to systems that merely digitize existing manual processes. This underscores the importance of incorporating analytics and decision support features to enhance the value proposition of agricultural information systems and ensure sustained usage by end-users.
In the Philippine context, Gonzales et al. (2022) observed that provincial agriculture offices increasingly require municipal-level systems to provide not just raw data submissions but also analytical summaries that support regional planning initiatives. Their study found that systems with built-in analytics capabilities reduced the analytical burden on provincial staff and improved the overall quality of agricultural planning across multiple municipalities. These findings directly support the fourth objective of the present study, which aims to develop an analytics and decision support module for the OMA of LGU Kinoguitan.


Integrated Crop, Livestock, and Fisheries Information Systems
Agricultural information at the municipal level extends beyond crop production. The Philippine Statistics Authority's Philippine Food Security Information System organizes food-security information under crops, livestock and poultry, and fisheries, while the PSA Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries database publishes statistical tables for these production domains (Philippine Statistics Authority, n.d.-a; Philippine Statistics Authority, n.d.-b). This sector grouping supports the expansion of AGRODATA into an integrated platform while recognizing that each sector requires distinct records, classifications, quantities, and production events.
A shared agricultural producer profile is important because a household may participate in more than one activity, such as crop farming, livestock raising, fishing, or aquaculture. A unified identity record reduces duplicate registration while sector-specific tables preserve the different workflows for farms and harvests, animal inventories, municipal fish catch, and managed aquaculture cycles. In AGRODATA, the existing farmer profile performs this shared producer function and links the individual to the applicable production records.


Livestock and Poultry Records Management
The Philippine Statistics Authority's technical notes on livestock and poultry statistics identify production volume, animal inventory, and slaughter or disposition as important measures for describing the sector (Philippine Statistics Authority, 2025). These measures demonstrate that livestock and poultry data cannot be represented accurately through crop planting and harvest records alone. Periodic inventory by animal species and location, together with births, deaths, dispositions, and production quantities, provides the OMA with a practical municipal record of animal activity.
For the present study, the livestock and poultry component records a producer, animal species, category, barangay, reference date, inventory count, births, deaths, disposed animals, and optional production type, quantity, unit, and notes. Validation prevents negative counts and quantities. The component is intended for inventory and production monitoring only and does not replace veterinary diagnosis, laboratory testing, disease surveillance, or individual-animal traceability systems.


Fisheries and Aquaculture Records Management
Philippine fisheries statistics distinguish commercial, municipal, and aquaculture production, while municipal fisheries may be described as marine or inland. This distinction supports separate capture-fisheries and aquaculture records rather than a single undifferentiated production entry. Capture-fisheries records identify fisherfolk activity, catch date, subsector, species, quantity, and unit, whereas aquaculture records require a managed site and a stocking-to-harvest cycle.
The Department of Agriculture-Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources' Philippine Fisheries and Coastal Resiliency Project emphasizes the importance of resilient fisheries and aquaculture development and the use of information in planning interventions (Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources, n.d.). The Food and Agriculture Organization also documents the integration of fish with crop and livestock farming and the importance of regular aquaculture recordkeeping for production management (Food and Agriculture Organization, n.d.-a; Food and Agriculture Organization, n.d.-b). These sources support the inclusion of fisherfolk, fish catch, aquaculture site, stocking, status, and harvest information in AGRODATA, while fisheries licensing, enforcement, and vessel tracking remain outside the system scope.


Summary
The review of related literature and studies reveals strong and consistent evidence supporting the development of AGRODATA for LGU Kinoguitan as an integrated agricultural data management system. The reviewed studies identify the same core problems addressed in this study: the absence of a centralized data bank, time-consuming and inaccurate report preparation, difficulty in retrieving production records, weak validation, and the limited availability of descriptive information for agricultural decision-making. Philippine statistical systems further demonstrate that municipal agricultural information should account for crops, livestock and poultry, and fisheries rather than treating agriculture as crop production alone.
      The literature on agricultural data banking confirms that centralized digital systems improve data accuracy, completeness, accessibility, and retrieval. Studies on automated report generation demonstrate that system-generated reports can reduce manual compilation and improve consistency. Literature on livestock and poultry statistics supports periodic inventory and production-event records, while fisheries and aquaculture references support separate fisherfolk catch records and managed stocking-to-harvest cycles. Research on agricultural analytics and decision support underscores the value of transforming validated records into descriptive totals, counts, averages, distributions, comparisons, and historical trends.
      The present study is distinct from the reviewed studies because it addresses these requirements in one web-based system designed for the OMA of LGU Kinoguitan, Misamis Oriental. AGRODATA uses a shared agricultural producer profile with specialized crop, livestock and poultry, municipal fisheries, and aquaculture records; server-side sector reports and computations; and common validation, role-based access, audit, and backup controls. The added sector fields and external report formats remain subject to validation against the actual forms and procedures of the OMA and concerned receiving offices.

METHODS AND MATERIALS
Research Design
This study employed the developmental research design, which is appropriate for studies aimed at designing, developing, and evaluating a specific product, system, or program intended to solve an identified practical problem. According to McKenney and Reeves (2021), research design involves the iterative design, development, and evaluation of digital systems and solutions, aimed at solving real-world problems while generating practical and theoretical knowledge. This design was selected because the primary purpose of this study is the creation and evaluation of the AGRODATA for the OMA of LGU Kinoguitan, which directly addresses the identified problems of the office.
The research approach adopted in this study is applied research, as it is directed toward solving a specific, real-world problem experienced by the Office of the Municipal Agriculturalist of LGU Kinoguitan. The study followed a systematic process of problem identification through consultation with the Municipal Agriculturalist, requirements analysis, system design, development, testing, and evaluation. This approach ensured that the developed system is closely aligned with the actual needs and operational context of the OMA.










Figure 2 
Agile Software Development Model
  

Agile is a software development methodology centered on iterative and incremental progress, where systems are created through small cycles called sprints. Each sprint delivers a functional component that can be tested and refined based on feedback. This method encourages flexibility, ongoing assessment, and close collaboration between developers and users. McKenney and Reeves (2021) note that Agile supports digital systems for practical problem-solving, while Kumar et al. (2023) highlight the importance of flexible development approaches for modern information systems, especially those managing dynamic data like agricultural records.  
The Agile model was chosen for this study because it allows continuous refinement of the Agricultural Data Management System in agricultural offices. Supporting this, Koldovskyi et al. (2025) and Radhakrishnan (2025) emphasized that adaptive and flexible methodologies are essential for creating effective e-government systems, reinforcing Agile is suitable for this project.
The choice of Agile for developing AGRODATA was a deliberate and practical decision based on the project's context. Since the system was intended for a government agricultural office with no prior experience in digital systems, the development team could not define all requirements upfront. Changes, clarifications, and new needs were expected throughout the process, making Agile the most suitable methodology to accommodate these evolving demands. Similarly, Batoon et al. (2023) faced this scenario when creating a farm data management system for a municipal agriculture office, noting that many operational requirements only became evident as users engaged with early system versions. Gonzales et al. (2022) had comparable findings when using Scrum to develop a web-based agricultural produced crops monitoring system, highlighting that regular sprint reviews allowed agricultural officers to observe progress and provide feedback that significantly improved the final outcome. 
Both studies illustrate that Agile is particularly effective in agricultural offices, especially at the local government level, because it frames system development as a collaborative process between developers and end-users. Instead of a one-time handoff of requirements followed by isolated coding, Agile encourages ongoing interaction and feedback. This approach was precisely how AGRODATA was developed, with the Municipal Agriculturalist and OMA staff actively involved in every sprint. Their continuous participation ensured that each delivered module met actual needs and was practical for use.

Location of the Municipal Agriculture Office
  
  

The study will be conducted at the Office of the Municipal Agriculturalist of the Local Government Unit of Kinoguitan, Misamis Oriental. Kinoguitan is a municipality located in the province of Misamis Oriental, Region X (Northern Mindanao), Philippines. Agriculture is an important source of livelihood in the municipality and includes crop farming, livestock and poultry raising, municipal fishing, and aquaculture. The OMA of Kinoguitan is responsible for overseeing agricultural activities and programs within the municipality and was selected as the research setting because it exemplifies the multi-sector data management challenges that this study seeks to address.


Data Gathering
The data gathering process for this study was carried out in two main phases to ensure a thorough understanding of the existing agricultural data management practices at the Office of the Municipal Agriculturalist (OMA) of LGU Kinoguitan.
In the first phase, the proponents held a formal consultation with the Municipal Agriculturalist to identify current data management practices, assess the limitations of the existing manual system, and gather initial system requirements. They also reviewed relevant documents, including producer-registration records, farm and crop forms, livestock and poultry inventory records, fisherfolk and fish catch forms, aquaculture records, and municipal or provincial reports. This document analysis provided the foundation for designing the system's shared producer record, sector-specific database tables, input forms, and report parameters.
In the second phase, follow-up consultations and informal interviews were conducted with OMA staff responsible for crop, livestock and poultry, fisheries, and aquaculture records to gain further insights into their workflows, challenges, terminology, units, reporting schedules, and expectations for the proposed system. The information gathered from these interactions was used to refine the system requirements. Data fields and report formats derived from secondary sources shall be confirmed against the OMA's actual forms before being treated as official requirements.


Research Instruments
This study will use a semi-structured interview guide, a document-analysis and data-field validation checklist, and the International Organization for Standardization/IEC 25010 software quality evaluation tool as the research instruments. The semi-structured interview guide will be used during consultations with the Municipal Agriculturalist and OMA personnel to understand crop, livestock and poultry, fisheries, and aquaculture data practices; identify existing challenges; and gather the functional and non-functional requirements needed for the proposed system. The document-analysis checklist will record each required field, definition, unit, source form, frequency, responsible encoder, reviewer, and report destination. These instruments will help validate the identified problems and prevent unsupported assumptions about sector forms or external compliance.
In addition, the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality evaluation tool will be used to assess the quality, functionality, and overall acceptability of the developed digital agricultural data management system. This evaluation standard is widely used in software development studies to measure whether a system meets user needs and performs efficiently. The evaluation will focus on key software quality characteristics such as functional suitability, performance efficiency, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and portability. 
The ISO/IEC 25010 evaluation questionnaire will be answered by the intended users of the system, including the Municipal Agriculturalist and OMA staff responsible for the covered agricultural sectors. Their responses will help determine whether the developed system is effective, user-friendly, secure, and capable of supporting integrated agricultural data management and decision-making processes. The results of the evaluation will also serve as the basis for identifying possible improvements and ensuring that the system meets acceptable software quality standards.
















System Design
The system design consists of as following;
Current System
Figure 4
Current process flow
  

            The current process flow starts when agricultural producers and field personnel provide crop, livestock and poultry, fisheries, and aquaculture information to the OMA. Staff manually record the information in forms, notebooks, logbooks, or separate files and store the records for safekeeping. When a municipal or provincial summary is required, staff return to the applicable sector records and manually compile the needed information, which takes considerable time and effort. The process does not provide one searchable producer record across sectors, automated report generation, persisted statistical summaries, dashboard visualization, or common validation. This makes the process slow and vulnerable to data loss, duplication, inconsistency, and difficulty in retrieving specific records. The absence of integrated analytical tools also prevents the office from readily comparing crop production, animal inventory, fish catch, and aquaculture activity for evidence-based planning.


Proposed System 
Figure 5
Proposed System Flow
  

The proposed AGRODATA: an agricultural data management system in agricultural offices will replace the fragmented manual data management processes of the OMA with a centralized, web-based platform covering crops, livestock and poultry, municipal fisheries, and aquaculture. Authorized OMA staff first search for an existing agricultural producer before registering a new profile. They then encode the applicable sector record: farm, plot, planting, and harvest information for crops; species, inventory, births, deaths, dispositions, and production for livestock and poultry; fisherfolk activity and catch by date, subsector, species, quantity, and unit for municipal fisheries; or site and stocking-to-harvest cycle information for aquaculture. The system validates required information, non-negative quantities, dates, and parent-child relationships before saving records to PostgreSQL and recording applicable actions in the audit log.
      The Municipal Agriculturalist reviews the encoded records and descriptive dashboards and generates reports through authenticated Supabase Edge Functions. The implemented report types are the Quarterly Crop Production Report, Seasonal Farm Inventory Report, Annual Municipal Agriculture Summary, Farmer Registry, Livestock and Poultry Inventory Report, and Municipal Fisheries Catch Report. Edge Functions execute database queries and return compiled report data for preview, PDF, Excel, CSV, or printing. The analytics function computes and persists crop yield, livestock and poultry, and fisheries summaries for monthly, quarterly, or yearly periods. Dashboard indicators also present recorded aquaculture harvest totals. These analytics describe recorded data only and do not forecast future production. External report layouts remain subject to validation with the OMA and receiving offices.

Figure 6
System Architecture of the Proposed System
  

The system architecture of AGRODATA follows a three-tier client-server model. The Presentation Layer is a React and TypeScript single-page application containing shared role-aware modules for producer profiles, farms and crops, livestock and poultry, fisherfolk and fish catch, aquaculture sites and cycles, analytics, reports, users, audit, and backup. OMA staff use the shared record-management pages as encoders, while Municipal Agriculturalist access controls protect reporting, user administration, audit review, and backup operations; separate duplicate pages for each role are unnecessary.
      The Application Layer uses Supabase authentication, auto-generated database APIs under Row Level Security for record operations, and Supabase Edge Functions for server-side report generation and statistical computation. TanStack Query manages frontend data fetching and caching. The Data Layer uses a normalized PostgreSQL database containing shared user and producer records and specialized crop, livestock and poultry, fisheries, aquaculture, and statistical-summary tables. Foreign keys, checks, indexes, role policies, and audit records support integrity and accountability. Processed data is returned to the Presentation Layer for chart rendering and report export.

Actor
	OMA Staff
	Use Case Name
	Register Farmer Profile
	Description
	This use case allows the OMA Staff to create and register a new agricultural producer profile within AGRODATA. The profile is the foundational identity to which farm and crop, livestock and poultry, fisherfolk and fish catch, and aquaculture records may be associated. A person who participates in several sectors retains one profile. Producers do not directly access the system; all registration is performed by authorized OMA Staff.

	1. The OMA Staff member is logged into the system with an active session.

  a. The staff member attempts to submit the form with missing required fields.
